The problem

Two computers, one setup, and a physical-cable-swap tax every time you switch.

You've got a laptop for work and a PC for everything else, both fighting for the same two monitors. Unplugging and replugging every time you clock in or out is slow — and if your laptop charges through its dock, disconnecting means losing power too.

MMH skips the swap. It runs on each machine and enables or disables the monitors connected to it with one click, so your screens instantly hand off between computers — no cables, no hardware KVM box, no fuss.

How it works

Three steps, one button.

01

Install on both machines

Clone, build, and run MMH on your work laptop and your personal PC — it's a lightweight Windows app, nothing to plug in.

02

Pick a layout

Click a config button — "Left Monitor" or "3 Monitors" — and MMH runs the PowerShell display commands behind the scenes.

03

Screens hand off instantly

The other computer's monitors free up automatically, so whichever machine you're on always owns the right screens.

Why not just buy a KVM switch

Hardware KVMs solve this too. Here's the trade-off.

 MMHHardware KVM switch
CostFree$100–$300+
Extra hardware on your deskNoneBox + extra cables
Works with your existing dockYesSometimes conflicts
Flexible layouts (not just A/B)Yes — mix & match monitorsUsually fixed A/B
SetupClone, build, runUnbox, wire, configure
High‑performance GPU support Full — monitors stay directly connected to your GPU Limited — bandwidth caps, reduced refresh rates, broken HDR/adaptive sync

Why this matters: Traditional KVM switches route your video signal through the KVM box itself, which means your monitors are no longer directly connected to your graphics card. That breaks high‑bandwidth features like high refresh rates, HDR, G‑Sync/FreeSync, multi‑monitor GPU output, and sometimes even basic stability. MMH avoids this entirely — your GPU stays wired straight to your displays, so you keep full performance while MMH handles only the USB/HID switching.
